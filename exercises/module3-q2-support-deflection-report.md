# Q2 Support Deflection Report

**Customer Operations — internal**
**Prepared for the Q3 planning review**

## Summary

The self-service knowledge base pilot launched on April 1 for the Atlas product
line. Over the quarter, inbound support tickets for Atlas fell 34%, from 8,200
in Q1 to 5,400 in Q2. We recommend expanding the knowledge base to all product
lines in Q3.

## Ticket volume

Atlas tickets by category:

| Category | Q1 | Q2 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Account and billing | 3,100 | 2,050 | −34% |
| Installation | 2,400 | 1,600 | −33% |
| Feature questions | 1,900 | 1,100 | −42% |
| Bug reports | 900 | 780 | −13% |
| **Total** | **8,200** | **5,400** | **−34%** |

The decline was broad across categories. Feature questions fell the most, which
is expected: that category maps most directly to the articles we published.

## What changed

We published 46 articles between April 1 and June 15. Search was added to the
help center on April 12. We also updated the in-product help links in the 4.2
release, which shipped April 30.

The 34% reduction in tickets is a direct result of the knowledge base. Customers
who previously would have filed a ticket are now finding answers themselves.

## User feedback

We surveyed Atlas customers in late June. 12% responded. Of those who responded,
81% rated the knowledge base "helpful" or "very helpful," and 76% said they had
used it instead of contacting support at least once.

Users say the knowledge base has substantially improved their support
experience.

## Cost impact

Average fully loaded cost per ticket is $18. At 2,800 fewer tickets, that is
roughly $50,400 saved in the quarter, or about $200,000 annualized.

Content production cost approximately $60,000 for the quarter, mostly
contractor time. We expect production costs to fall sharply once the initial
article set is complete, though we have not yet modeled the ongoing maintenance
requirement.

## Caveats

The pilot covered Atlas only. Atlas customers skew toward larger enterprise
accounts with dedicated administrators, who may be more willing to self-serve
than our small-business customers on other product lines.

Q2 is also historically our lowest-volume quarter. Ticket volume fell 11% from
Q1 to Q2 in each of the last three years, before any knowledge base existed.

One large enterprise customer, representing approximately 6% of Atlas ticket
volume, migrated off the platform in early May.

## Recommendation

Expand the knowledge base to all product lines in Q3, with a target of 150
articles by year end. Based on the Atlas results, we project a comparable 34%
reduction in ticket volume across the remaining product lines, worth
approximately $740,000 annually.

We recommend funding a permanent content team of three writers.
